The Ghost Protocol: A Token Story

📅 October 12, 2024 👤 Avinash Mehta ⏱️ 5 min read

After three years of building systems that millions of people rely on every day, I've decided to step away. This isn't a goodbye post – it's a documentation of something I discovered that made me question everything about the systems I helped build.

⚠️ Disclaimer: This post contains technical details about transit system vulnerabilities. Specific identifiers have been obfuscated. This is for educational purposes only.

The Discovery

It started with routine debugging. A token validator at STATION_NAME was showing unusual patterns – accepting tokens that should have been flagged as already used. Digging into the logs, I found something unexpected: the validation checksum was being bypassed for tokens with specific serial prefixes.

Someone had introduced a backdoor. And it wasn't accidental.

What I Found

The backdoor allowed anyone with knowledge of the prefix pattern to generate unlimited valid tokens. We're talking about a system that handles ₹500+ crore in daily transactions. I reported it through proper channels, but something didn't feel right about the response.

"Sometimes the only way to protect a system is to prove it's broken."

The Protocol

I created a backup of everything – logs, code snippets, internal communications. I called it the "Ghost Protocol" because the evidence needed to disappear from their systems while still existing somewhere safe.

I can't work for an organization that prioritizes reputation over security. I can't pretend everything is fine when millions of commuters are using a system with known vulnerabilities.

To my former colleagues: I'm sorry it had to be this way. To those who will investigate this: the evidence exists. You just have to know where to look.
